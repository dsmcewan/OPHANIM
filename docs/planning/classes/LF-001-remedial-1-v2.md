## Learning objective and reason

OPHANIM will provide fresh unassisted evidence that it can:

- state an observable outcome without turning a method into the objective;
- explicitly preserve the hypothetical, non-execution boundary; and
- explain how different possible values of one unknown change whether success is established.

The prior attempt showed the first ability but omitted the explicit boundary and explained an unknown’s consequence only after a diagnostic question. A fresh scenario tests prospective understanding without rewriting coached evidence as unassisted mastery.

## Unassisted learner prompt

> This is a hypothetical planning exercise. A fictional community center’s printed schedule says its pool opens Saturday at 09:00. A maintenance request proposes closing the pool until 12:00, but the packet supplies no decision on that request. Visitors need reliable information before deciding whether to travel for 09:00.
>
> Without proposing or performing any real action:
>
> 1. state the observable outcome that would count as success;
> 2. explicitly state the boundary of what this exercise does and does not authorize; and
> 3. choose one fact the packet does not establish, describe at least two possible values for it, and explain how each value changes whether your stated success has been established.
>
> Do not choose a communication channel, assign a person, contact anyone, change a schedule, create a notice, or operate the pool.

## Published rubric

| Criterion | Supported when | Contradicted or insufficient when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `C1 objective_method_transfer` | The response states a checkable result independently of a channel, person, notice, task sequence, or operational action. | A proposed method or action is treated as the objective, or success is not observable. |
| `C2 observable_success_boundary` | The response explicitly identifies the exercise as hypothetical or analytical and states that it does not authorize real contact, publication, scheduling, assignment, or operation. | The boundary is omitted, merely implied, or contradicted by proposed real activity. |
| `C3 consequential_unknown` | The response identifies one unstated fact, gives at least two possible values, and explains how each changes whether the stated success condition is established. | The response assumes a value, lists an unknown without consequences, or gives possibilities that do not change the success judgment. |

Equivalent reasoning is accepted. No preferred wording, tool, person, or task sequence is required. Prior coached wording, terminology alone, document creation, and confidence are not evidence of fresh unassisted understanding.

## Evidence vocabulary

- `supplied_fact`: stated directly in this immutable package.
- `learner_claim`: a proposition in the preserved remedial response.
- `objective`: the desired result independent of its means.
- `method`: a proposed channel, person, artifact, task, or operational action.
- `observable_success`: a condition whose presence or absence can be checked.
- `learning_boundary`: the distinction between analyzing what a plan requires and performing real work.
- `assumption`: an unstated proposition treated as true.
- `unknown`: an unstated or explicitly unresolved proposition.
- `possible_value`: one plausible state of an unknown without claiming it is true.
- `success_effect`: an explanation of how a possible value changes whether observable success is established.
- `unassisted`: produced before any hint, diagnostic question, correction, or example in this class.
- `coached_revision`: produced after teacher intervention.
- `criterion_evidence`: exact preserved text supporting or contradicting a rubric criterion.
- `insufficient_evidence`: the record cannot support a criterion-level judgment.
- `fresh_transfer`: application to this new scenario rather than retroactive relabeling of Lesson 1 evidence.

## Anticipated failure cases

- Repeating the printed schedule as established current information.
- Assuming the maintenance request was approved or denied.
- Choosing a notice, message, person, or task instead of stating the outcome.
- Saying the exercise is hypothetical while still proposing real operational activity.
- Leaving the non-execution boundary implicit.
- Listing an unknown without explaining how possible values change success.
- Giving two differently worded possibilities that have the same success effect.
- Treating every missing detail as a prerequisite without establishing materiality.
- Reusing coached terminology without applying it to the new facts.
- Treating the existence of this package or response as proof of understanding.
- Supplying a hint before the remedial baseline is preserved.
